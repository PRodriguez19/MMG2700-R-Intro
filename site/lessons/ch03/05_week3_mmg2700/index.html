    Lesson 5: Team-Based Learning (TBL)

    Throughout the semester, we will use Team-Based Learning (TBL) activities to practice interpreting and communicating scientific data.

    These activities are designed to move beyond simply following instructions or producing an R script. You will practice the skills that scientists use when reading research papers, analyzing data, and communicating results: making observations, interpreting evidence, evaluating alternative explanations, and defending your conclusions.

    Before Class: Individual Pre-Work

    Paper to be discussed for today's class is Blackmore.et.al.2017

    Each TBL session will include preparation that must be completed before class.

    The pre-work is intentionally designed to prepare you for the team activity. You should complete the assigned reading before coming to class.

    Your preparation may ask you to:

    • Read and interpret a scientific paper OR review article.
    • Answer questions about an analysis or visualization.
    • Identify patterns or unexpected results.
    • Develop an initial interpretation of the data.
    • Identify questions or uncertainties that you would like to discuss with your team.

    Come to class prepared to defend your answers. You do not need to have the "perfect" interpretation before class. In fact, disagreement between team members is useful when it leads you to examine the evidence more carefully.

    During Class: Team-Based Analysis (~45 minutes)

    Time Activity
    45 mins Team-Based Analysis (groups of 3)
    30 mins Scientific Figure Recitation

    At the beginning of the TBL activity, you will work with your team to compare your individual interpretations and complete a written team assignment.

    Your team should:

    • Return to the data or figure to determine what evidence supports each interpretation.
    • Identify areas where you agree and disagree.
    • Reach a team conclusion.
    • Record your reasoning on the team worksheet.
    • Each member of the team is required to discuss at least one panel during the recitation portion. Please make assignments during this time so that the figure recitation is equitable.

    Scientific Figure Recitation (~30 minutes)

    Approximately 30 minutes of each TBL class will be devoted to whole-class discussion and recitation. This is where you will be graded for participation.

    During this portion of class, teams will be asked to come to the front of the room and discuss a figure, table, or analysis.

    When presenting, you should be prepared to:

    • Point directly to the figure or relevant result.
    • Identify the pattern or observation you are discussing.
    • Explain what the pattern means biologically or statistically.
    • Identify the evidence supporting your interpretation.
    • Explain any limitations or alternative interpretations.
    • Respond to questions from your classmates and instructor.

    You should think of this as explaining the data to a scientific audience, not giving a formal presentation.

    For example, rather than saying:

    "There is a difference between the two groups."

    You should be able to say:

    "In Figure 2B, the treated samples show higher expression of these genes than the control samples. This suggests that the treatment may be associated with increased activation of this pathway. However, we would need to consider __X_ before concluding that the treatment directly caused this change."

    Our Goals

    By the end of the TBL activities, you should be able to:

    • Interpret scientific figures and data rather than simply describe them.
    • Identify the main biological or scientific question being addressed by an analysis.
    • Connect an analysis or visualization to the question it is intended to answer.
    • Use evidence from figures, tables, and statistical results to support a conclusion.
    • Evaluate whether a conclusion is adequately supported by the evidence.
    • Discuss alternative interpretations of a result.
    • Explain your reasoning clearly to your classmates.
    • Work collaboratively to evaluate and defend a scientific interpretation.
    • Communicate scientific results using appropriate terminology and visual evidence.
